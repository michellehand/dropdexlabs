01 / THE PERSON BEHIND THE ARCHIVE

FROM THE ARCHIVIST / PERSONAL LOG

I DIDN'T SET OUT
TO BUILD AN ARCHIVE.

“
What if the thing we feel most intensely in music could also be explored through data?
— THE QUESTION THAT STARTED DROPDEX

I've always been drawn to the moments in bass music that feel bigger than the speakers producing them. A drop can change the energy of a crowd in an instant — but not every drop does it the same way.

My background brings together two ways of looking at the world: art and data analysis. One taught me to pay attention to feeling, form, and detail. The other gave me tools to investigate patterns and ask better questions.

DropDex began when those two interests collided. I started treating drops as events worth studying — not just listening to — and building a system to examine the differences between them.

What started as an experiment became a collection of measurements, classifications, rankings, and eventually physical cards. I call the person behind the research The Archivist.

This is an independent project, built out in the open, one record at a time. The system will evolve. That's part of the point.

04 / THE INVESTIGATION

HOW THE SYSTEM WORKS

THE DROP IS
THE DATA.

DropDex isolates the build, transition, and impact of a drop, then examines measurable characteristics of the audio. The model produces a repeatable record — not a verdict on anyone's taste.

01 / ISOLATE

FIND THE MOMENT.

Identify the build and the point where the drop begins, then define the analysis windows around the event.

02 / MEASURE

EXAMINE THE SIGNAL.

Study energy, spectral balance, attack, density, texture, tension, and release across the event.

03 / CLASSIFY

GIVE IT AN IDENTITY.

Summarize the acoustic profile through scores, classes, and signature traits.

04 / ARCHIVE

KEEP THE RECORD ALIVE.

Compare each specimen with the growing collection. Rankings can change as new evidence enters the archive.

THE PROCESS IS PUBLIC.
THE FORMULA IS NOT.

DropDex explains what its model evaluates, what its scores represent, and how its classifications behave. Exact weights, internal transformations, and scoring coefficients remain proprietary.

Results are model-based measurements, not objective judgments of musical quality. Every ranking reflects the current archive and can change as the dataset expands. The investigation is ongoing.
