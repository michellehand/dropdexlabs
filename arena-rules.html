Battle Protocol

Core Rules

ACM-3 does not simply compare total scores. Each card must perform across a sequence of acoustic combat phases.

01

Equal Starting Integrity

Every specimen enters with the same integrity. Archive rank does not grant additional health.

02

Five Combat Phases

Each battle can last five rounds. Every round emphasizes a different part of the drop.

03

Initiative Opens the Fight

Shock, aggression, attack behavior, class identity, and tension influence which specimen acts first.

04

Momentum Builds

Build strength, class behavior, traits, and successful exchanges increase pressure in later rounds.

05

Production Stabilizes

Production improves structural resistance and reduces volatility. Better-produced drops perform more consistently.

06

One Survivor

A knockout ends the match immediately. Otherwise, the specimen with the most integrity after Round 5 wins.

Five-Round Sequence

The Battle Phases

The Arena follows the evolving structure of a bass drop rather than repeating the same damage calculation five times.

Round 01

Initiation

The first collision. Fast, aggressive, high-shock specimens can establish an immediate advantage.

Shock · Aggression · Vacuum Tension
Round 02

Pressure

Momentum begins to influence the fight. Strong builds and controlled force start shaping the match.

Build · Flow · Momentum
Round 03

Control

Structure, production quality, density, and complexity determine which specimen commands the exchange.

Production · Texture · Density
Round 04

Endurance

The Arena tests staying power. Sustain, weight, groove, and accumulated pressure become increasingly important.

Sustain · Weight · Groove
Round 05

Finale

The final exchange rewards tonal control, scale, harmonic payoff, class escalation, and surviving momentum.

Tonality · Cinematic Scale · Momentum

Combat Identity

How Classes Fight

Classes shape how a specimen's effectiveness rises, falls, or remains stable across the five rounds.

Impact Cannon

Opening Force

Hits hardest in the early phases, then gradually loses its initial advantage.

Vacuum Striker

Surprise Attack

Uses tension and release to dominate initiation, but loses efficiency in extended combat.

Pressure Engine

Late Takeover

Builds pressure over time and becomes increasingly dangerous with every surviving round.

Groove Driver

Momentum Control

Gains strength after establishing rhythm and carrying momentum between phases.

Technical Weapon

Controlled Precision

Maintains stable output with lower performance variance throughout the entire battle.

Filth Monster

Persistent Damage

Carries aggressive texture and steady pressure through multiple phases of the match.

Cinematic

Final-Round Threat

Begins slowly, survives, and becomes most dangerous during the closing phases.

Melodic Weapon

Tonal Escalation

Uses tonal movement and harmonic payoff to become stronger in later phases.

Primary and Secondary Classes

A specimen's primary class defines its main combat curve. Its secondary class adds a smaller supporting influence.

Signature Behavior

Traits Decide Close Battles

Signature traits act as smaller acoustic modifiers. They do not replace the five scores, but they can determine the result when two specimens are closely matched.

Shock

Improves opening pressure and initiative behavior.

Vacuum Tension

Strengthens the release into the initiation phase.

Flow

Helps preserve momentum between combat phases.

Density

Improves structural resistance against sudden pressure spikes.

Sustain

Preserves effective pressure during endurance and finale.

Groove

Rewards rhythm control and consecutive strong phases.

Brightness

Helps cutting energy penetrate structurally stable opponents.

Texture

Makes a specimen more difficult to counter cleanly.

Harmonic Richness

Improves tonal payoff and late-round consistency.
